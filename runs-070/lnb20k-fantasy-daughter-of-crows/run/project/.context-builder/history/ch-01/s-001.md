### logline  <novel/outline/logline.md>

Rue, who sold herself into the Mercy Academy, spends a lifetime inside the institution that annually accepts one hundred girls but returns almost none; when war reaches the Academy's gates in her old age, buried bargains and the Academy's true price are forced into daylight, and Rue must reckon with what she once bargained away.

### entity-roster  <characters/, locations/, objects/>

Every entity that exists in this story. Only these ids may be used; if you need someone or something that is not here, say so rather than inventing an id.
- char-rue: Rue — A woman who sold herself into the Mercy Academy as a young woman. Quiet, practical, and watchful, she spends decades inside the Academy and grows into its reluctant conscience.
- collective-the-hundred: The Hundred — The Academy's annual cohort: one hundred girls acquired each year and folded into its programs. They are both a number and a shifting constellation of friendships, rivalries and losses.
- collective-the-three: The Three — The near-mythic set of alumni who, historically, have left the Academy after ten years. They are spoken of like miracles and warnings at once.
- char-headmistress: Headmistress — The senior administrator and public face of the Mercy Academy. Polished, diplomatic and possessed of practical ruthlessness; she embodies the Academy's benevolent rhetoric and its hard edges.
- char-war-envoy: War Envoy — A representative of the approaching war: formal, implacable and strange. He/they arrive with offers and threats that make the Academy's hidden arrangements surface.
- loc-mercy-academy: Mercy Academy — A high, walled institution on an island quartered between harbor, rose gardens and glasshouses. It calls itself sanctuary and school; its walls hold more than classrooms.
- loc-great-hall: Great Hall — The Academy's ceremonial heart: where the Hundred are received, where names are read into the folio and where the Headmistress meets visitors.
- loc-ink-room: Ink Room — A locked room below the library where the Academy's ledgers and the binding inks are kept. Staffed by a small, generational guild of scribes.
- obj-contract: Contract — The ledger contract each recruit signs. On its face it promises education and safety in return for ten years; its margins hold clauses not printed for patrons.
- obj-folio: Folio — The Academy's ceremonial book that bears the names of every girl who entered and every name that was allowed to leave. It is heavy, stained and glossed with old inks.
- obj-amber-bell: Amber Bell — A small bell rung whenever a cohort is closed or when a departure is recorded in the folio. Its sound is both celebration and seal.
- obj-binding-seal: Binding Seal — A wax-and-ink sigil used in finalizing contracts; it carries a sorcerous tether that makes some of the Academy's claims enforceable.
- char-aya: Aya — A bright, quick-witted recruit in Rue's first cohort at the Academy; she becomes Rue's close friend and an emotional anchor early in Rue's life there.
- char-loren: Loren — An older instructor who teaches craft and discipline to the Hundred. Stern but humane, he mentors Rue across decades and becomes one of the few inside the walls whom she trusts.
- char-elaine: Elaine — One of the named individuals counted among the Three. She left the Academy years ago and lives beyond the city; she carries a scar the Academy never explains.
- char-maris: Maris — Another of the Three. She left under different circumstances and is used by the Academy as an example of success.
- char-sal: Sal — The third of the Three, quieter and more evasive; rumours say she left with secrets the Academy does not want repeated.
- obj-archive-key: Archive Key — A small iron key, stamped with the Academy's crest, used to open the deepest cabinets in the Ink Room and the library's sealed stacks.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- The Academy accepts exactly one hundred girls into a cohort each year; this number is public and rigid in the story's social systems.
- Historically, after a ten-year period inside the Academy, only three individuals have left and their departures are recorded in the Folio (this historical fact is established early and treated as canon unless explicitly changed by a scena-level event with provenance).
- Contracts the recruits sign are legally and magically binding when sealed with the Binding Seal; the Seal enacts the Academy's claims but requires the Academy's own rituals to be maintained.
- Magic has a cost: any enchantment that binds a person's freedom draws from the same ledger as the Folio and must be accounted for in the Academy's records; bargains cannot be silently created or negated without record.
- The War Envoy represents external political/military forces; he may demand terms, levy burdens, or threaten force, but he cannot invent or erase Academy records — only expose and exploit them.
- The Folio truthfully records entries and departures; it can be read and misread, hidden, or weaponised, but altering historical entries is itself an action with consequences and must be shown on-page when it happens.
- The Headmistress is powerful within the Academy but not omnipotent; she must work within political constraints, her staff and the Academy's rituals.
- No deus ex machina: solutions to the Academy's bindings must arise from revealed rules and the characters' established resources and relationships, not from unexplained new powers.
