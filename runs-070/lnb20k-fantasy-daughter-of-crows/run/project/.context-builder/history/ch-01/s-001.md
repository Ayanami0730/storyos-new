### logline  <novel/outline/logline.md>

Rue sold herself to the Mercy Academy as a young woman and spends her life inside an institution that takes one hundred girls a year and releases almost none; when a war finally reaches its gates in her old age, Rue must reckon with the bargains she made and the Academy's hidden realities as the past and the present collide.

### entity-roster  <characters/, locations/, objects/>

Every entity that exists in this story. Only these ids may be used; if you need someone or something that is not here, say so rather than inventing an id.
- char-rue: Rue — entered the Mercy Academy by selling herself as a young woman. Quiet, practical, stubborn; who grows into a skilled keeper of the Academy's work and into an old woman who knows every hidden stair.
- group-hundred: The Hundred — the yearly cohort: one hundred girls accepted into the Academy each year. They are treated as a unit in ritual and accounting, but each life within it differs.
- group-three: The Three — the rare survivors who walk back out after ten years. They are legendary both inside and outside the Academy; in living memory there are only three named exits.
- char-headmistress: Headmistress — the Academy's senior administrator and public face. Polished, almost merciful in speech, steeped in ceremony; she maintains the Academy's bargains and its outward story.
- char-war-envoy: War Envoy — a representative or manifestation of the war that arrives at the Academy's gates during Rue's old age. Part diplomat, part omen; they speak with the cadence of marching feet.
- loc-mercy-academy: Mercy Academy — the sprawling institution on the hill that accepts one hundred girls each year. A city of wings, cloisters, gardens, and ledgers; its facades promise comfort, its inner courts conceal rules.
- loc-dormitory: Dormitory — the long dormitory halls where the Hundred are lodged on their first night and where bonds and rivalries are formed.
- loc-chapel: Chapel — the high, fan-vaulted room where the Academy performs its vows and binding rites; where the bronze bell and amber lantern are used.
- loc-archives: Mercy Archive — the locked room of records and ledgers: the Contract Folio, the Academy Ledger, correspondence with the outside world.
- obj-contract-folio: Contract Folio — a thick leather folio in the Archive that records every girl, the terms of her admission, and the signatures that bind her. It is both legal document and symbol.
- obj-binding-bell: Bronze Bell — rung at the conclusion of binding rites. Its toll marks the moment the Academy claims a newcomer; its sound is woven into the institution's myths.
- obj-amber-lantern: Amber Lantern — a small ritual lantern carried during induction, used to light a girl's first steps into the dormitory and to witness her signature.
- obj-red-thread: Red Thread — a length of dyed thread the Academy uses in certain ceremonies to mark service and to stitch dates onto cloths; it has become a shorthand for staying/going.
- faction-kingdom: The Kingdom — the political power beyond the Academy's hill. It is the source of the war and of the edicts that shape the Academy's permitted behavior.
- char-warden: Warden — a middle-ranking officer of the Academy who supervises the Hundred's daily routines and enforces rules with a mix of firmness and small kindnesses.
- char-ida: Ida — one of the Three who left the Academy. Her face is known in rumor; she left ten years after her entrance and carries a private wound and an explanation she does not like to give.
- char-marra: Marra — another of the Three. She left with a rumor of miracle and theft; she appears in the public imagination as either saint or thief, depending on who tells the tale.
- char-lyss: Lyss — the third of the Three. She left quietly and vanished into the wider world. Her story is the most contested inside the Academy, held up and down as a warning and a hope.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- The Academy acquires exactly one hundred girls each year; this is enacted publicly at induction and recorded in the Contract Folio. This must be shown in early scenes and reiterated as ritual fact.
- Entry into the Academy is contractual and recorded in the Contract Folio; signatures and ritual (the Amber Lantern and the Bronze Bell) are the formal mechanisms of transfer. The Folio's entries are binding within the society's recognized law and in the Academy's internal enforcement mechanisms.
- After a ten-year period from an individual's entry, historical record shows only three individuals (the Three) ever left the Academy. The text must present this as a factual, auditable statistic in the world; any alternative explanations (escape, false death, cloakings) must be consistent with the archive's records and cannot contradict the three-exit fact without being explicitly recorded as a retcon in the Archive.
- The Academy is not supernatural in the sense of all-powerful magic; it uses law, ritual, social control, and institutional networks to enforce its claims. Any mystical or uncanny elements must be limited and optional; the story hinges on institutional power and human choices.
- The War is an external political-military force from the Kingdom. The War Envoy is its representative. The war can bring violence and political demands to the Academy's gates; it can break archives and expose secrets but cannot arbitrarily rewrite history—the Contract Folio's entries remain legible unless physically destroyed, and any such destruction must be shown in scene provenance.
- Characters' ages and timelines must be coherent: Rue enters as a young woman, ages across the book, and is convincingly old by the time the war arrives. The Academy's calendar and the ten-year mark must be narratively consistent.
- Any departures, disappearances, or deaths inside the Academy must be recorded in the Archive unless the tale explicitly shows an attempt to erase or alter those records. Marginal notes, contradictions, and redactions in the Folio are permissible but must be presented with provenance (whose hand, what scene caused it).
