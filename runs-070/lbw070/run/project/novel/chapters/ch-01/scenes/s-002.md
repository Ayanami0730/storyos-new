We step off the stoop and onto Main Street; the street smells like oil and heat, like a dragon that hasn't quite fallen asleep. Jun's shoulder bumps mine as we move, his hand curled around the straps of his backpack. He's got the tin safe in there and the flashlight heavy and familiar in his pocket, off and quiet.

Main Street is worse up close. "Orange cones march in a crooked line; faded sawhorses have 'BRIDGE CLOSED' hand-lettered across their planks. A rumble of trucks keeps the air tasting of oil; the detour sign points the other way and a gap between cones is narrow and muddy—the boards are split, nails up like teeth. Cars hiss past at the crosswalk, each one a gray wave." I say the last part under my breath because truth makes a better story when you tell it quietly.

A boy leans against the opposite railing, chewing something with the casual cruelty of lunchtime kings. Milo. He has the smirk that thinks being loud is the same as being brave. "Oh look, knights on a quest—don't get eaten by a bus, Eloise," he calls. Laughter tacks onto the end like a banner.

I feel the map fold beneath my palm even if it isn't out. In my head Main Street becomes a moat, the cones a string of crocodiles. Jun is the only person who hears the real danger and the imagined one in the right order; he watches the traffic and watches me at the same time.

"We go around—under the bridge repair, like we planned," I tell Jun, because plans are the cords that keep a kingdom from falling apart. He nods, fingers worrying the flashlight in his pocket. The tin thumps against his spine in the pack; I can feel the velvet pouch against my thigh where the brooch sits, a small warm weight I do not let go of.

Milo steps forward as if that will make him bigger. "You'll chicken out," he says, but he steps toward the curb too, because bullies like an audience. Cars hiss; a truck rolls by and the air shakes.

Jun doesn't argue. He scans a path between cones that looks like it was made for boots, not for children. "Hold tight—watch the curb, now!" he snaps, the practical shout that keeps us from doing something stupid, and I obey. We pick our way over the mud, the boards tremble under our sneakers and the nails look like teeth waiting for the careless foot. My heart clips like it's running to keep up.

Halfway across, my sneaker slips and mud kisses the heel. Jun's hand catches my elbow—hard, sure—and steady. He doesn't make a show of it. He just pulls me level, the map folded in my chest like a quiet treaty.

We reach the other side with our knees dusty and our shoes marked. Milo's laugh has died down. Jun pats his pocket as if to check the flashlight is still there; it's still off, a small, obedient dark. The tin is where it should be, and the brooch presses against my leg. The moat is crossed. The next stretch of road waits, and for once the world feels properly big.
