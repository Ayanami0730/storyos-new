### previous-scene  <novel/chapters/ch-01/scenes/s-001.md>

I come home to find Jun on our stoop, one foot propped on the rail like he’s guarding an entrance to a kingdom. He looks up as I drop my backpack and there, on the top step, is Mrs. Halloway with a tin balanced on her knee. The world does a neat thing: small, urgent errands always arrive with a quiet gravity.

She smiles at me like she’s choosing a messenger. "Eloise, darling, would you take this lunch to my sister? And would you mind giving her my silver brooch? It’s... meant to find its way home." She presses the tin into my hands like a benediction.

I hold it and let the sun warm the metal. "A dented tin lunchbox, its knight's paint flaked at the edges, smelling faintly of tomato and pennies; inside, a paper-wrapped sandwich and a small velvet pouch." The words taste like instructions. I slide the pouch closer to my palm, feel the thrift-shop cool of silver beneath the fabric.

Mrs. Halloway stands, brushes dust from her skirt and turns toward the sidewalk, leaving a little line of her in the air behind her—an image I can call up any time: "A tidy blue door set on a low porch where ferns droop in green pots; the porch boards smell of lemon oil and old tea." I tuck that picture in my head because it is how I will know when we have arrived.

Jun arches an eyebrow at the tin. "What is it?" he asks.

"Quest supplies," I say, and unfold the map with a small ceremonial flourish. The paper is folded until the creases ache; my finger finds the village like a needle finding a thread. "The map is honest where it matters: names, monsters, and the one true shortcut." That is the legend, carved into the margin in my worst handwriting.

He reads the squiggles—Main Street is a moat, the cracked pavement a field of shifting stones, Mrs. Halloway’s bungalow a blue tower with a ferned gate. Jun is practical in ways I admire: he counts risks the way other people count sheep. "Can we cross Main Street?" he asks.

I look down the block like I’m sizing up a dragon and decide dragons are less scary with a plan. "We go down the block, under the old bridge repair. It’s slower, but the dragon’s asleep there." I say it with the tone that turns cardboard into cliff.

He snorts and pockets the flashlight I call the Sword of Light. He tucks the tin into his backpack so it doesn’t rattle. I clip the velvet pouch with my thumb and feel the weight of the promise in my pocket, the brooch like a small, stubborn thing that wants only to be home.

Jun and I stand on the sidewalk, the map folded between us like a treaty. The sun is a coin, the day short and right for a quest. We step off the curb together, which is our way of saying yes.
