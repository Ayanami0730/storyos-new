The porch complains when Arden sets the leash down—one long creak like a page turning—and the dog leans into him, warm and trusting beneath his palm. Mrs. Blythe opens a small velvet-lined tin on the hall table with a careful, ceremonial motion, lifts out the brass collar, and shows it as if it were an heirloom. "Keep it safe," she tells him, voice small and certain. "It's been with us a long time." Arden nods and slips the tin into his bag, feeling the weight of brass and a small, mysterious scratch like a scar.

Outside, the world smells of damp cardboard and yesterday's rain. The cobbles are uneven underfoot; the dog pulls at the leash toward a gutter where a moth flutters like a pale flag. Hollis is already there when Arden reaches the bottom step—half in the lane, half in a comic bow, eyes bright. A canvas satchel is slung over Hollis's shoulder, and with a flourish Hollis produces a flattened square of cardboard, all inked landmarks and dramatic arrows, and a lacquered broom handle with a paintbrush tied to the end.

"Map," Hollis announces, as if unveiling a crown. The cardboard smells faintly of glue. The broom handle is the sort that should be sweeping porches, not starting wars, but Hollis has taped and painted it until it looks like something older. "And Excalibroom. For balance and dramatic entry."

Mrs. Blythe fusses with the leash. The dog wags, a bright center of movement. Arden eases the collar from its velvet tin and snaps it onto the brass ring of the dog's tags. It catches on the metal for a second, then—one quick, traitorous slip—the collar flips free and arcs through the air. It hits the pavement with a single metallic ring and bounces toward the gutter, a small sun the size of a coin.

Hollis's mouth makes a perfect grin. "A quest," Hollis says, as if there could be no other truth. "The King's Sigil suffers treachery. We must fetch it."

The children crouch so they’re the same height as the collar. Hollis taps the cardboard map with the stub of a finger, then taps the brim of an imaginary crown made from a scrap of paper and tape. Hollis plasters the coronet to Arden's hairline as if bestowing rank. "Sir Arden," Hollis declares, voice deep and ridiculous, "by the Star, we fetch the King's Sigil and bring it back." Hollis offers a fist; Arden bumps it with his own.

Arden feels the oath settle in his chest—ridiculous, heavy, the way a coat does when you put it on and it fits. He realizes, absurdly, that he has Mrs. Blythe's knotted rope tucked into his belt loop because she insisted on some precaution, and the coil is warm against his hip. The collar glints in the gutter; for a moment it is only bright metal, then a symbol. Arden says, quieter, "We bring it home." Hollis grins, tight and glad. The dog snuffles at the coin-size brass, uninterested in crowns.

They step off together, Hollis's map held like a banner, the broom-handle staff tapping once against a cobble. The street has become their quest route before they reach the end of the block.
