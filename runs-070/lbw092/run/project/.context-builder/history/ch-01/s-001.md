### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- Social hierarchy is rigid: nobles may contract marriages; servants have no legal claim to marriage with nobility and cannot inherit titles or land.
- The lord's authority within his household is near-absolute: he can order expulsion, binding betrothals, and disinheritance; his signet ring is the instrument of household legal acts.
- The Church's rites (a priest's presence for a marriage or vow) are required for a marriage to be legally recognised. A private 'binding' without a priest has no standing.
- No supernatural or anachronistic elements: maintain physical realism and plausible period social mores.
- Letters, tokens and objects are admissible as evidence in household disputes but may be ambiguous — provenance must be credible if later cited as fact.
- Deaths and legal consequences follow realistic procedures (inquest, household inquiry) and will have social repercussions (loss of standing, rumour).
