### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- The story is set during World War II–era Hong Kong (Japanese occupation era); technology, institutions, and social constraints must be period-appropriate and not anachronistic.
- Supernatural mechanics: spirits exist and interact with humans, but their strength is tied to human memory, trauma, and communal denial rather than undifferentiated magical fiat.
- Rituals have material and social costs: a successful rite requires ingredients, consent or participation from the living, and can have tangible side-effects (psychological, physical, or social) on individuals and the community.
- No deus ex machina: Mercy cannot simply 'erase' the spirit with a single magical trick; resolution requires investigation, negotiation, and communal confrontation of historical events.
- The revenant’s power is amplified by wartime violence: historical atrocities, suppressed confessions, and collective silences feed it; the spirit is not merely evil but a wound given form by historical neglect.
- The Walled City’s geography matters: tight spaces, layered dwellings, roofs, and shared courtyards affect how rituals operate and how the community responds.
- Ethical constraint: exorcisms may harm the living—physically, economically, or socially—and these harms must be acknowledged and shown in consequences.
- Characters’ memories and oral histories are treated as evidence: discoverable, mutable under the spirit’s influence, and central to resolving the conflict.
