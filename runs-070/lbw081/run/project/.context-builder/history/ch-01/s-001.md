### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- No supernatural or impossible mechanics; all explanations must be physically plausible.
- Forensic facts are realistic: time-of-death estimates, fingerprints and mud/DNA behave as they would in a small-country-house setting.
- The first-person narrator (char-detective-marcus) cannot know others' private thoughts; observations and motives must be inferred and later confirmed by evidence or confession.
- All entities used in scenes must be declared in this plan; the writer may introduce no new character, location, or significant object without updating the plan.
- Locked-room constraints are literal: exterior doors and the study window are latched or bolted from the inside in the initial state; any remote or delayed locking must be shown in physical detail and repeatable.
- No deus ex machina: the solution must be reproducible by the detective with the objects available on scene or clearly accessible (ladder in the shed, garden access, mantel clock, matches, thread).
