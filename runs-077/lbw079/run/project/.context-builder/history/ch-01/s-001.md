### logline  <novel/outline/logline.md>

First-person narrator moves into an old Victorian house and uncovers objects tied to its previous tenant. As reflections and physical traces accumulate, the narrator must untangle a decades-old injustice to stop a grief-driven haunting — a confrontation that forces an admission of truth and an ambiguous release.

### entity-roster  <characters/, locations/, objects/>

Every entity that exists in this story. Only these ids may be used; if you need someone or something that is not here, say so rather than inventing an id.
- char-alex: First-person narrator (early 30s). Practical, a little exhausted by life, moved into the Elm Street house to restart; skeptical until events force belief.
- char-hargreaves: The landlord who rented the house to Alex; mid-50s, polite, slightly evasive; knows the house's history more than he admits.
- char-mrs-ellison: An elderly next-door neighbour who remembers the house's previous occupants and offers reluctant warnings and scraps of local lore.
- char-isaac: The ghost — Isaac Hallow, the house's previous tenant. Alive in memory and documents; when manifesting he is focused, accusatory and grief-filled rather than monstrous.
- loc-elmhouse: The Victorian house on Elm Street: creaking floors, narrow stair, small attic. The house itself feels like a single, watchful thing.
- loc-living-room: Downstairs room with a mantle and an old mirror over it; where the narrator first notices odd reflections and the house's rhythm.
- loc-bedroom: Alex's bedroom on the second floor with a porcelain mirror and one loose floorboard; claustrophobic ceiling and thin walls.
- loc-attic: Dusty, low-ceilinged attic with trunks, an old dresser with a locked drawer, and the strongest smell of the past.
- obj-rusty-key: A tarnished iron key given to Alex by Hargreaves. It opens a back drawer and the attic hatch; it feels colder than it should.
- obj-porcelain-mirror: An old, slightly pitted wall mirror in the bedroom. Its glass sometimes shows small discrepancies from reality — delayed motions, extra figures.
- obj-wristwatch: A brass pocket watch stopped at a specific minute. It belonged to Isaac and was hidden under a loose floorboard.
- obj-photograph: A faded black-and-white photograph of Isaac with a small girl, edges browned. Found tucked beneath the loose floorboard.
- obj-wooden-floorboard: A loose board in the bedroom floor that conceals the photograph and watch. It creaks in a distinct way that later signals presence.
- obj-locked-drawer: A small locked drawer inside an old dresser in the attic. It contains a folded letter and a brittle ledger.
- obj-letter: A folded, ink-smudged letter from Isaac, written the week before his death. It speaks of fear and names no one directly but references a 'keeper'.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- The ghost (char-isaac) is bound to the house (loc-elmhouse) and to objects that belonged to him (obj-wristwatch, obj-photograph, obj-porcelain-mirror). It cannot leave the property unless an object that carries its charge leaves with someone.
- Supernatural effects are tied to perceptual conduits: reflections (mirrors), memory-holding objects (photographs, watches), and locations that preserve repeated gestures (the attic, the loose floorboard). Phenomena will be repeatable and consistent: the same creak, the same stopped minute, the same mirrored motion each time they occur.
- Documents and physical evidence found in the house are treated as accurate records of past events for the purposes of the story. A letter or ledger discovered in the attic is a canonical source; any claim derived from them must be recorded as such in the prose.
- The ghost cannot invent facts or create new historical evidence; it can only re-embody past moments, gestures, fragments of speech and sensory impressions that tie to its grievance.
- The narrator (char-alex) is a reliable witness of their perceptions. Hallucination as an explanation is permitted in the narrator's internal doubt, but the story's logic treats the phenomena as real in-world; contradictions must be explicitly resolved by scene evidence.
- Resolution must address the ghost’s revealed grievance (acknowledgement, naming of a responsible human, or removal of the binding object). There is no deus ex machina: any method of release or containment must operate within the rules above (e.g., moving the watch off-property moves the ghost's charge, naming the responsible party satisfies the need for truth).
