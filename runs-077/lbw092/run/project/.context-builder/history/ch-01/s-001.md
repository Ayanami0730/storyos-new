### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- No supernatural or anachronistic elements: all events must be realistic and historically plausible within a late-feudal/early-modern aristocratic household.
- Class and legal rules constrain marriage: a servant cannot lawfully marry the lord's daughter without the lord's explicit consent; betrothal sealed by the lord and priest is socially and legally binding within the story's community.
- The lord's signet and a waxed seal constitute admissible proof of household orders and letters; misdelivery or tampering with sealed correspondence can be interpreted as evidence by household authorities.
- Servants have limited access to private family spaces; evidence of impropriety (tokens in private quarters, misdelivered family letters) is treated as a serious offence and triggers formal inquiry.
- Poisons and draughts exist as ordinary, non-magical substances with lethal potential; their use has realistic physical consequences and legal/moral implications in the story.
