### scene-card  <novel/chapters/ch-01/chapter.yaml>

Scene s-001. Intent: Open on the narrator arriving at Crowe House after the coroner has left. Establish mood (damp evening, exacting eye), present the locked-room puzzle with precise evidence: a study locked from the inside, the antique clock stopped at 2:17, the torn letter in Edmund's hand, key missing from the lock, window latched, a single set of muddy bootprints outside. The narrator records sensory detail, meets Inspector Hart and Omar, and feels the puzzle's shape for the first time.

Target length: about 700 words.

### logline  <novel/outline/logline.md>

A private investigator called to a sealed study where a reclusive collector has been murdered faces a classic locked-room: door locked from the inside, window latched, clock stopped at 2:17. As interviews and experiments narrow the field, the narrator must outthink an argument that used time and an object meant to order it as a murderer's tool.

### entity-roster  <characters/, locations/, objects/>

Every entity that exists in this story. Only these ids may be used; if you need someone or something that is not here, say so rather than inventing an id.
- char-narrator: First-person narrator and private investigator (mid-40s). Ex-detective turned PI; wry, exacting, easily bored. Keeps careful notes and narrates events in present-tense memory with professional reserve and a private grief that motivates the case.
- char-edmund-crowe: Victim. Reclusive antiquarian and collector of clocks (late 60s). Owner of Crowe House; meticulous, wealthy, habitually precise about time and keys.
- char-lydia-crowe: Edmund's niece (early 30s). Smart, urbane, outwardly calm; stands to inherit if Edmund dies without a will change. Says she was at a dinner party across town the night of the murder.
- char-omar-klein: Long-time housekeeper and caretaker for Crowe House (50s). Quiet, loyal, knows the mansion's quirks and maintenance secrets. Says he discovered the body and called the police.
- char-daniel-kai: Neighbor and fellow collector (40s). Friendly rivalry with Edmund over rare pieces; arrives later that evening claiming he went to borrow a book and found the road blocked.
- char-dr-ines-mali: Medical examiner assigned to the case. Direct, clinical; concise about time and cause of death in ways that tighten the timeline rather than loosen it.
- char-inspector-hart: Local police inspector. Professional, courteous to the narrator but territorial. Ran the initial scene and left the room sealed until the coroner finished.
- loc-crowe-mansion-study: The locked room. A compact, wood-paneled study on the ground floor at the rear of Crowe House. Heavy oak door with an old brass lock; single sash window with an internal latch; desk faces the window; mantel with an antique clock above it.
- loc-crowe-mansion-foyer: Entrance hall and route to the study. Wide, shadowed in the evening; coat hooks and a front door that is often left unlocked for guests.
- obj-antique-clock: An ornate mantel clock Edmund prized. It had stopped at 2:17 when the body was found. The clock is mechanical and old; it can be wound and its casing hides a shallow false back.
- obj-brass-key: Small brass skeleton key of the old oak lock on the study door. At discovery the official record notes the study's door was locked from the inside and the key was not in the lock.
- obj-torn-letter: A torn piece of stationery found in Edmund's right hand; fragment contains a few words in a tight, nervous hand and an ink blot. The rest of the letter is missing when the scene is first examined.
- obj-ink-bottle: An antique bottle of iron gall ink kept on Edmund's desk. Labeled and dusty; a thin ring of dried ink sits on its lip. It is a plausible medium for a poisonous application if tampered with.
- obj-window-latch: The internal latch on the study's sash window. Appears undisturbed on first examination; the sash is latched from the inside and the exterior is unreachable without a ladder.
- obj-bootprints: A single set of muddy bootprints on the flagstone path leading to the rear of the house; recorded in the initial scene log. Patterned, large, possibly from work boots.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- No supernatural explanations: every locked-room solution must be human (mechanical, psychological or misdirection) and physically plausible.
- Forensic and timeline details must be internally consistent and match ordinary forensics (time of death windows, temperature effects on a stopped clock are not supernatural).
- The narrator is first-person and not omniscient: they do not know other characters' private thoughts unless observed or reported; claims on motive must be supported by evidence in the scenes.
- All characters, locations and objects used later will be drawn only from the stable ids declared above; new entities must be added through the plan-update process.
- No identifiable real persons or public figures are used or referenced.
