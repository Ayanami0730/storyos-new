When Mara returns to her childhood house to settle her mother's affairs, a walnut mirror in the attic keeps showing a girl who isn't her — a ghost tethered to a brass locket and to being seen — and the more Mara tries to prove it's a trick, the more the house's reflections conspire to make her the only one who can remember Evie, perhaps at the cost of herself.

- char-evie: Evie Ashford, the ghost. A girl who died decades ago; her presence is tethered to a small brass locket and to reflections in a particular walnut mirror. She remembers being seen and named, and she demands witnesses.

- obj-brass-locket: A small, tarnished brass locket on a fragile chain. Inside is a tiny sepia photograph of two girls on a shore; the locket is Evie's most persistent tether to the world of the living.
- obj-faded-photo: A sepia photograph, larger than the one in the locket, of two girls standing on a marshy shore. Someone, long ago, wrote a name on the back in faded ink: 'Evie.'
- char-noah: Noah Whitcomb, an elderly neighbor who remembers the house and the girl named Evie. He is stubborn and blunt, keeps to his own property across the lane, and provides a few key facts about the past — but not all of them.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- The ghost is anchored to objects of memory (the brass locket and the faded photograph). Those objects, while intact, allow the ghost to fix and replay a moment of its life via reflective surfaces.
- Reflections are the ghost's active domain: mirrors and other clear reflections can show the past as a layer over the present and can carry limited sensory impressions (voice, cold, a touch through the glass), but they do not automatically permit the ghost to 'step' into the physical world.
- A ghost cannot leave the property unless it has a physical tether placed on it in the outside world (for example, the locket buried at the marsh). Returning a tether to the place tied to the tether can either sever the ghost's hold (freeing it) or strengthen the ghost's claim, depending on whether the act is done as a named remembrance or as an attempt to hide/forget it.
- Physical destruction of the mirror severs the reflection-channel but does not by itself destroy the ghost; it redirects the ghost's attention to other reflections and, if done without the remembering ritual, can enrage the ghost and make it more dangerous.
- Naming matters: saying the ghost's name aloud while the tether and a reflection are present is a ritual act that changes the balance — it can either grant the ghost a 'witness' to be satisfied by (calming it) or allow it to take stronger hold of the namer if the namer's attention falters or they lie to themselves.
