### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- 1) Ghosts are anchored to unresolved actions, places, or singular objects; they cannot manifest independently of their anchor (the mirror and the doll) except as atmospheric effects.
- 2) The mirror is a conduit for preserved perceptions (past 'frames') not a time machine: it can replay scenes exactly as they occurred (sensory fragments, emotions, and sound) but cannot alter physical records or create demonstrably new facts.
- 3) Manifestations can influence living perception (cold, scent, auditory hallucination) and can move small, unsecured objects under specific, narratively-established conditions (e.g., when the lullaby plays), but they cannot levitate large heavy furniture or perform impossible physics.
- 4) Technological recording devices (cameras, audio recorders) may register anomalies differently from human perception: sometimes capturing nothing a human sees, sometimes capturing sounds humans missed. This mismatch is consistent and explainable within each scene.
- 5) A ghost's 'release' requires acknowledgement of the unresolved action (truth spoken or an act that symbolically completes the missing ritual). Physical destruction of an anchor (breaking the mirror) can sever an attachment but at a cost and with predictable local consequences — it will not erase the narrative consequences already written in diaries or records.
- 6) No retroactive contradiction: nothing revealed later can contradict a committed textual or physical piece of evidence already established in the scenes (e.g., dates in the diary, public record facts). Any new revelation must be consistent with earlier, documented facts or explain how the earlier facts were misunderstandings.
