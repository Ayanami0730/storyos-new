### logline  <novel/outline/logline.md>

On the single platform of her hometown, a young artist about to leave for art school says a quiet goodbye to the place and people who raised her; a carved wooden boat and a dog stand between the life she leaves and the one she will make, and a small, ordinary blessing from an old neighbor gives her the courage to go.

### entity-roster  <characters/, locations/, objects/>

Every entity that exists in this story. Only these ids may be used; if you need someone or something that is not here, say so rather than inventing an id.
- char-ellie: Ellie, 19, a small-town girl with paint-smudged fingers and a stubborn, gentle steadiness. Leaving Marlow for art school but tied to the town by memory and a promise.
- char-mr-hart: Mr. Hart, late 60s, retired mechanic and neighbor; knits without fuss, carves wood in his shed, and has been a quiet guardian figure in Ellie's life.
- char-rufus: Rufus, a limping stray dog Ellie adopted, patchy fur and an old collar; follows her everywhere and understands small rituals.
- loc-marlow-platform: The single-track rail platform at the edge of Marlow: low brick wall, a battered bench, and the view down the main street toward the sea. The town's small departures happen here.
- loc-marlow-mainstreet: Marlow's main street: a line of low shops, the bakery with its bell, the postbox, and the narrow lane leading toward Mr. Hart's house and the platform.
- obj-notebook: Ellie's sketchbook: a soft-covered, cloth-bound notebook with corners bent and the spine taped where pages once threatened to fall out. Inside are drawings of small-town details and notes she never shares often.
- obj-carved-boat: A small, hand-carved wooden boat, varnished smooth, the work of Mr. Hart. He gave it to Ellie when she was a child; she has carried it as a talisman for years.
- obj-suitcase: A worn, grey suitcase patched with duct tape and a strip of blue cloth from Ellie's mother. It carries the practical and sentimental fragments she's willing to take.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- No supernatural events or magical realism: everything must be explicable in ordinary terms.
- Character ages and identities are fixed by their entity sketches and must not be contradicted later (e.g., char-ellie is 19; char-mr-hart is late 60s).
- Objects retain continuity: an object given in one scene (e.g., obj-carved-boat) exists in future scenes and may carry wear or notes explaining its condition.
- Geography is consistent: Marlow is a small town with a single rail platform and a main street as described — distances and directions should not be contradicted.
- No retroactive retcons without an explicit index entry: any change to these facts must be recorded in the project's continuity files with provenance.
