### logline  <novel/outline/logline.md>

When a fast-moving zombie virus erupts in Willow Creek, a small group of residents led by Ellie, the town mechanic, make a desperate bid for the highway. As the town collapses into noise and hunger, loyalties fray and sacrifices are made — Ellie records the run in her notebook and dies fighting to buy her friends a chance at escape.

### entity-roster  <characters/, locations/, objects/>

Every entity that exists in this story. Only these ids may be used; if you need someone or something that is not here, say so rather than inventing an id.
- char-ellie: Ellie Rivera, 28, town mechanic and narrator. Practical, stubborn, keeps a small leather notebook she writes in. She is the POV and dies fighting to buy her friends a chance to escape.
- char-dan: Dan Mercer, 30, former volunteer firefighter. Steady under pressure, good with engines and plans. He becomes the group's driver and one of the survivors.
- char-maya: Maya Alvarez, 25, EMT trainee. Calm, medically knowledgeable, morally determined to keep people alive. She survives the escape.
- char-otto: Otto Beck, 67, retired high-school teacher. World-weary, kind, physically fragile but mentally sharp. He does not survive the outbreak.
- char-lina: Lina Beck, 16, Otto's granddaughter. Brash and quick, still learning restraint. She is caught out in a panic and is killed during the escape.
- loc-willow-creek-town-square: The small town's center; stores, the clock tower and the market. Where news and rumor move fastest and where the outbreak first becomes obvious.
- loc-gas-station: A corner gas station with a small convenience store and a single working pump. The group's first rallying point and the place they fortify to make initial plans.
- loc-hospital: Willow Creek Community Hospital: a one-story hospital with an ER, limited supplies and a locked pharmacy. The group risks it for medicines and morphine.
- loc-old-bridge: An aging wooden bridge over the river on the road to the highway. Structurally fragile and later found blocked/collapsed — forces the group to change route.
- loc-rail-yard: An abandoned rail yard on the town's outskirts that the survivors plan to cross as an alternate route toward the highway; open, exposed and dangerous at night.
- loc-riverbank: The river's muddy bank below the old bridge and beside the rail yard. A place of concealment and desperate choices.
- loc-highway: The interstate beyond town that represents safety and a route out. Reaching it is the group's goal; it may or may not be clear.
- obj-truck: A dented 1997 Ford pickup (Dan's truck) with a busted tail-light and a stubborn diesel engine. The group's principal means of escape.
- obj-shotgun: A pump-action 12-gauge shotgun kept in the truck's cab. Reliable at close range but with limited ammunition.
- obj-radio: Battery-powered handheld radio with several cracked channels; intermittent contact to outside. Used for scraps of news and as a lure.
- obj-medkit: A small EMT bag Maya carries: dressings, morphine, a tourniquet and limited antibiotics — worth risking a hospital run for.
- obj-pickaxe: An old pickaxe from the hardware store used as an improvised melee weapon and to force open doors.
- obj-flare: A single red flare saved in Ellie's pack. Loud, bright, and intended as a last-ditch diversion or signal.
- obj-gas-can: A half-full jerry can used to refuel the truck and for bargaining fuel. Precious in a town running out of gasoline.
- obj-journal: Ellie's small leather notebook and pen — her way of making sense of what happens and the device that frames the first-person narration.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- Transmission: the virus spreads only via direct bites or contact between infected blood and an open wound. Saliva alone without break in skin is not reliable transmission.
- Kill rule: an infected host stops moving only when the brain is destroyed or irreparably damaged. Torso or limb destruction alone does not stop motion.
- Incubation and timeline: symptoms appear within 2–6 hours of exposure; collapse to violent, disoriented aggression follows quickly. The outbreak escalates from quiet to town-wide within a day.
- Behavior: the infected are drawn to loud noises and sudden movement. They show no planning, forethought, or tool use — purely stimulus-driven aggression.
- No cure, no supernatural: there is no miraculous immunity, cure, or resurrection. Bodies do not reanimate after being truly destroyed (brain destroyed).
- Resources are limited: fuel, ammunition and medicine are scarce; scavenging is risky. Vehicles need fuel and basic mechanical function to move; the truck can be jury-rigged but not made into something it is not.
