### previous-scene  <novel/chapters/ch-01/scenes/s-001.md>

My shoelace came loose and for a heartbeat the morning threatened to fold in on itself; then my fingers found something warm beneath the cereal box.

I hauled out a dented metal lunchbox whose handle left a smear of jam across my palm. The heat on the metal felt like it had just met the sink; somewhere a kettle hissed and the toaster clicked. The backpack strap dug into my shoulder like an impatient elbow and a slant of light from the narrow kitchen window cut the counter into a bright river. Books leaned against the far wall, their spines like quiet sentries; flour dust freckled the surface where Mara had been practicing pancakes and the smell of browned batter hung in the air.

Mara was at the counter, cheeks dusted with flour and grinning as if she’d been waiting for an errand to become important. She popped the lid and held the lunchbox up with mock solemnity. "Behold: the Crown of Crumbs," she said, as if presenting a crown at court. Inside was Finn's ham-and-cheese sandwich, three thin apple slices tucked in a corner, and a tiny cardboard juice box with a cartoon fox smiling at me.

She didn't waste time on ceremony. She grabbed the back page of my math homework and wrote, in a hurry that somehow still looked decisive: "Quest List." Then she folded the paper into a flag and read aloud: "1. Cross the moat (bridge). 2. Pay the toll (Mr. Bennett). 3. Return Crown to Finn at the schoolyard." She handed me the flag like it was the map that mattered.

From under the cereal box she produced a cardboard helmet — battered, the Frosty O's logo ghosted along its side, a strip of silver duct tape across the dented brow, and a crease where someone's elbow had made a valley. She clipped a small blue plastic compass to the kitchen's old keyring; the glass over the face was spidered but the needle still swung, stubborn as a promise.

There were twelve minutes until the bell. Saying the number out loud made our feet decide for us.

Mara appointed roles before I could think too long. "You're the Bearer of the Crown," she announced, tapping the lunchbox into my palm. "I'm Keeper of Maps. If anyone tries to steal our quest, I'll distract them with a sonnet. Or with very dramatic eyebrows."

The lunchbox felt absurd in my hands and also exactly right, like a weight that taught you how much you cared. For a second I pictured Finn waiting under the pine tree in the schoolyard, small and stubborn and convinced he didn't need anyone's help — and that turned the ridiculous into something necessary.

We moved through the kitchen with the practiced silence of people trying not to miss a thing: don't trip, don't spill the juice box, don't bang into Mr. Bennett on the path. Mara shoved the cardboard helmet onto my head like a coronation and hooked the compass to my zipper. The Crown of Crumbs bumped against my side as we stepped onto the sidewalk; the King's Road waited.

Outside, the morning smelled like wet pavement and possibility.
