Scene s-001. Intent: Scene 1 (≈500 words): Establish the everyday task and set up the quest. In Eli's narrow kitchen (loc-eli-house) he discovers the forgotten obj-lunchbox while tying shoes. When Eli realizes he's forgotten his little brother Finn's lunch, he and his imaginative friend Mara turn the ordinary errand into a heroic Arthurian-style quest across 'moats' and 'toll keepers' — and discover that bravery, friendship, and small acts of responsibility can make a kingdom of the everyday.

- char-ms-ramirez: Ms. Ramirez (30s) — the patient, watchful schoolyard supervisor who notices small acts of courage and names them aloud.
- loc-eli-house: Eli's narrow, book-lined house on Maple Lane; the story opens in its cramped kitchen where the forgotten lunchbox is discovered.
- loc-suburban-path: The route from Eli's house to school: sidewalks, hedges, a cracked flagstone path that Mara renames 'The King's Road.'
- loc-bridge-over-creek: A short wooden footbridge over a shallow creek that the children imagine as a moat; a plank is loose and the crossing becomes a test.
- loc-schoolyard: The tarmac playground and cluster of trees behind Roosevelt Elementary where Finn waits and the quest is completed.
- obj-lunchbox: The lunchbox (nicknamed the 'Crown of Crumbs') — Finn's sandwich and juice tucked inside; the quest's objective and a talisman of responsibility.
- obj-cardboard-helmet: A battered cardboard helmet that Mara fashions into a 'knight's helm' from a cereal box; it frames the children's play and courage.
- obj-quest-list: A scrawled list on the back of a homework sheet where Mara writes down 'quests' and rules — their contract for the day.
- obj-compass-key: A small toy compass attached to a keychain — half-broken, mostly decorative, it becomes their 'true north' when decisions are hard.
- obj-rope: A short length of climbing rope Eli carries on his bike (leftover from a family camping trip) used to improvise a safe crossing.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- No supernatural magic: imaginative transformations are metaphor and shared play that change how characters perceive events, not physical reality (e.g., a creek is still a creek).
- Characters' ages, relationships and basic facts must remain consistent across scenes (Eli = 12, Mara = 12, Finn = 7; Eli and Finn are siblings; Mara is Eli's friend).
- Time flows realistically: the trip must fit a school morning's timeline; actions (walking, talking, rigging rope) take plausible amounts of time.
- Consequences are real: injuries, lateness, or scolds can happen; bravery does not exempt characters from ordinary results.
- All objects and locations introduced here are available for use later; no new characters, locations, or significant objects may be introduced without adding them to the index first.
