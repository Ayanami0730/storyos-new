I asked them to come back in the morning. Daylight makes liars smaller and mechanisms larger; Milne arrived with his notebook like a blunt instrument, Mary with her hands folded as if she might lay them over the truth, Oliver with his face the colour of peat, and Henry as composed as a man who expects a room to agree with him.

I set the window as I had the night before: a short loop of cord through the little metal eye in the ivy, the letter-opener lashed to it so the haft hung true. I placed a cloth against the sash to take the bite; I put the clock where the hands could no longer be trusted and told Milne to time me with his watch. They watched where people watch at trials — not at the instrument, but at the person who uses it.

My hands are better at small betrayals than at speeches. I threaded the cord through the loop again, felt the loop take the weight with a little give, and let the opener rest in my palm. The brass was cold; it filled my hand the way a small animal fills a glove. I thumbed the gap on the sash — a thumb's breadth, just enough; the glass yielded like skin. I pushed the opener through, feeling the resistance of the cloth as it closed about metal. The haft slipped, the oil on it catching the light like a dry promise.

There are sounds no one notices until you make them matter: the shush of cord through ivy, the faint drag as metal meets seal, the cloth's brief, surprised surrender. I drew the opener free, wound the cord a single, sure turn, and with a small pull the sash settled as if it had never been moved. Milne's watch ran the seconds out loud; when I let my fingers go he said the number into the air: "Less than a minute." It was the sort of shortness that unmakes alibis.

I did not let the demonstration speak for the whole case. I laid the things out on the desk: the folio with the ragged gap where a page had been torn cleanly away; the smear of oil that ran to the folio's edge and stopped; the handkerchief from Lena's palm, its corners blood-darkened. Under daylight the monogram was still smudged and unreadable — no tidy initials caught the eye. I did not pretend the cloth named him; its letters were, for all the room's clear light, illegible.

I placed the ashtray where it had been; the household recognised the brand by habit even if the laboratory had not stamped it for the file. The oil on the opener matched the smear by the folio in the way that a fingerprint matches a face: not absolute proof, but a joining of small things. Whoever could make the sash open from outside with a loop in the ivy and an obedient weight could thrust a blunt instrument through the gap and withdraw it without leaving the window visibly disturbed. Whoever could do that would know to smudge oil on the haft and on a page to make the trace look like toil rather than malice.

It was Henry who answered the chain I had laid. He had kept his face like a ledger's cover for too long. Milne asked the question I wanted him to: "Where were you between ten and half past?"

Henry's gloves came off in a small, quick gesture. "I went to her window," he said. The words came out flat, like a coin on a table. "I couldn't stand her tallying me — the things she had written. She loved that folio more than she loved me. I meant to go, to frighten her, to make her stop — I didn't think it would be like that." His hand shook, as if he had only just felt how heavy the opener had been.

Henry admitted, on the record, that he feared the ledger's entries about him and that fear had driven him to act. He told them how he'd fixed the loop in the ivy, knotted the cord, made the opener a weight and a blade for the same motion, and how he'd pulled the sash neat again. He admitted the torn page. "I took the sheet out. I could not have it in her book. It would have ruined me." In his words the quarrel over money and the will took a sharper shape: not only dispute, but dread of a ledger that would read him bankrupt in reputation.

Milne's notebook closed with a dull, practiced noise. He did not find the case tidy; he found it finished. He stepped forward when Henry's anger ran to a thin, legal thing and took him by the wrist. Mary said nothing at first; Oliver's hands were empty of tools but full of relief. I thought then of small proofs and larger truths: the demonstration had made the how visible; a man's admission had made the why indisputable.

Milne wanted neat words for the file. I wanted the sort of certainty that does not fit neatly on a form. I let him have the paperwork. For once, paper and blunt instruments agreed.
