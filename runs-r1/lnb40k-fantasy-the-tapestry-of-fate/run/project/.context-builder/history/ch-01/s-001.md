### logline  <novel/outline/logline.md>

Coerced by an immortal peri council and bound by a silver token, pirate captain Amina al-Sirafi must sail to the inescapable Island of Qarrah and steal a single spindle capable of rewriting fate — a heist through shifting woods, memory-lands, and moral thorns that forces her to choose between her freedom and the lives she would reorder.

Every entity that exists in this story. Only these ids may be used; if you need someone or something that is not here, say so rather than inventing an id.
- char-amina: Amina al-Sirafi, a pragmatic, legendary pirate captain known for her seamanship, sharp wit, and a private code of honor. She is fiercely independent and haunted by past losses.
- faction-peri-council: An immortal council of peri—winged, otherworldly beings—who govern certain human affairs and hoard subtle metaphysical leverage. They are patient, inscrutable, and capable of making bargains that bind mortals.
- char-peri-azad: Azad, a senior member of the Peri Council who speaks for them. Charming, cold, and ceremonial; he is the council's interlocutor with mortals and the face of its coercion.
- char-peri-sahr: Sahr, an austere peri councillor who keeps records of bargains and penalties. She carries the council's memory of promises and debts.
- loc-port-rahim: Port Rahim: A bustling, sunbaked port city on the edge of the known sea where Amina's ship is docked and where merchants, sailors, and the supernatural brush shoulders.
- ship-simorgh: The Simorgh, Amina's ship: a sleek lateen-rigged vessel, fast and well-armed, home to her crew and the immediate stage for the journey.
- char-laila: Laila, Amina's first mate and childhood friend: steady, quietly fierce, and devoted to Amina. She trusts Amina's judgement but warns when lines are crossed.
- char-nasir: Nasir, the ship's quartermaster and an old salt with a pragmatic streak. He knows the sea's moods and keeps the crew's ledger and grudges.
- char-ibrahim: Ibrahim, a young helmsman with nimble hands and an appetite for stories. He is eager to prove himself and often reads omens in weather and birds.
- loc-open-sea: The open sea between ports and dangers: mutable, full of currents, storms, and occasional supernatural phenomena shaped by peri handiwork.
- loc-island-qarrah: The Island of Qarrah: an island steeped in fog and enchantment, inhabited by witches. Locals say no one who goes there can return; the island rearranges exits and memory.
- faction-witches-qarrah: The Witches of Qarrah: a secretive sisterhood that tends the island's uncanny geometry and preserves knowledge of fate. They bind newcomers in rituals and keep the island's laws.
- char-mother-khayla: Mother Khayla, the eldest and strongest of the Qarrah witches: sharp-tongued, ancient, and fiercely protective of the island and its artifacts.
- char-elif: Elif, a young witch who questions the island's strictures. Curious and empathetic, she becomes a hinge character between Amina and the coven.
- obj-spindle: The Spindle: a small, intricately wrought spindle of unknown metal and thread that can twist and re-weave strands of fate. Its workings are dangerous, precise, and morally fraught.
- obj-peri-token: A silver token issued by the Peri Council: a binding talisman that enforces bargains. Held to a mortal throat it polices obedience with a slow, inexorable pressure.
- obj-map-of-ways: A ragged chart of currents and ley-lines—part map, part enchantment—given to Amina by the peri. It points paths to Qarrah and hints at the island's counter-magic.
- char-tariq: Tariq, a former scholar turned deckhand who loves books and keeps secrets. He translates old marks and helps read the island's inscriptions.
- char-gul: Gul, a charismatic trader in Port Rahim who recognizes the peri token and warns Amina of the council's patience and reach.
- loc-witch-grove: The Witch Grove: a shifting sanctuary on Qarrah where the coven weaves fate with plants, roots, and songs. It is both laboratory and temple.
- loc-ash-scar: The Ash Scar: an unnatural valley on Qarrah where travelers' memories thin; gestures and names erode there, making escape nearly impossible.
- obj-witch-lode: A bowl of black thread and iron needles kept in the Grove: the coven's tools for minor rewrites and for measuring the spindle's influence.
- char-amina-ghost: An apparition of Amina's late sibling (a memory-ghost that appears in dreams and in the island's mirrors). It is part memory, part enchantment, and a voice that tempts and tests her.

### narrative-voice  <novel/style/voice.md>

Narration: third person limited, Amina, past tense. This was decided for the whole book before any of it was written and does not change.
Check your own sentences against it before you stage them. Mixing a collective "we" into a third-person narration, or slipping into present tense for a scene that feels immediate, is scored as a defect and is the most common one this system has produced: seven of nine consistency errors in a measured 18,000-word manuscript were exactly this.
If you think the story genuinely needs a different voice, propose the deviation — do not simply write it.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- Peri are immortal and can bind mortals with tokens that enforce obedience in graduated stages; the token cannot be destroyed by ordinary means but can be 'unbound' by specific fate-work.
- The Spindle can rewrite fate only in precise, threadlike edits. Every change demands a proportional cost: a memory, a bond, a trade of consequence that cannot be fabricated.
- The Island of Qarrah enforces inescapability by altering memory, geography, and perception; physical exit is possible only when memory-anchors remain or fate-work alters the island's weave.
- Witches of Qarrah practice confined fate-work (repairs and calibrations) and guard artifacts. They will not allow wholesale erasure of whole peoples or broad cosmic retcons.
- Peri bargains are durable across time; their enforcement is metaphysical and not subject to mortal courts, but the principles of exchange (offer, counteroffer, price) are consistent and can be exploited by skilled fate-work.
- Magic has rules and friction: artifacts like the Spindle are not omnipotent; the more ambitious the rewrite, the more localized and invasive the cost. Miracles are narrow and expensive.
