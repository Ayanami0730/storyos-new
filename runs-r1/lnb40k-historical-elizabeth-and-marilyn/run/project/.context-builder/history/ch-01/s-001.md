### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- The story is set in the summer of 1956 in Windsor; period technology and social institutions (no mobile phones, no internet, reliance on telegrams, print press, radio) must be respected.
- The novel must avoid inventing major public actions or official events for the Queen that contradict established historical fact for 1956; invented private interactions and small domestic details are allowed.
- The private meeting between the Queen and the actress is fictional but must be plausible within 1956 protocol and social norms; no implausible modern behaviours or anachronistic speech.
- Any claim about press coverage, photographs, or public reaction must be plausible for the pre-digital, 1950s British and international press environment.
- Objects and entities listed in the plan are the canonical identifiers; later scenes and state records must only refer to those ids and no new primary characters, locations or significant objects may be introduced without updating the plan.
- The narrative voice is third person omniscient and will occasionally enter interiority but will not claim access to unverifiable private thoughts beyond what characters plausibly guard.
- Secrecy is to be treated seriously: no scene may present a public, verifiable record of the private meeting; any physical proof (photograph, object) must be ambiguous, contested, or carefully controlled within the story.

Guidance: When staging a private walk in 1956 Windsor, the writer may choose between two plausible approaches (recorded as choices rather than facts):

1) Formal discretion: A lady-in-waiting accompanies the sovereign on foot; a small number of household staff (a footman or butler) remain in a visible-but-distant position, timed to intercept if needed. 2) Intimate privacy: The Queen slips away escorted only by a trusted lady-in-waiting; household staff are informed later to maintain secrecy, with the butler responsible for timing meals and ensuring the Queen's absence does not disrupt state calls. This fits a Queen who values privacy and trusts inner staff.
