Write a complete fantasy novel of about 60,000 words.

Premise:
A third-generation book-witch who upholds the fragile laws that govern fictional worlds can slip into any novel and act where its characters and narratives falter. When her beloved imaginary detective — a character she has admired since childhood and who exists only inside the stories she polices — volunteers to partner with her, they set out to solve two intertwined mysteries: the sudden disappearance of her grandfather, a former guardian of the archives, and the theft of an odd, powerful codex that should never have been on a shelf at all. Their investigation carries them through shifting genres and cramped pages, from gaslit alleys to high castles, forcing her to balance the cold duty of an enforcer with the messy loyalties of family and the strange intimacy of loving a being who is at once fiction and ally. As they chase clues across narrative borders, they uncover a pattern of erasures and contraband editions that threaten both the stability of story-worlds and the fragile boundary between fiction and the world she calls home.

Central conflict:
She must reconcile her official duty to police and preserve the integrity of novels with a desperate, personal hunt to find her missing grandfather and reclaim a stolen, dangerous book, while the alliance with her favorite fictional detective blurs the line between professional restraint and emotional attachment.

Characters:
- Mira Vale — the third-generation book-witch protagonist who can step into novels to police and repair narratives.
- Inspector Rowan Hale — the protagonist's cherished fictional detective partner, clever and shaped by the conventions of the mysteries he inhabits.
- Elias Vale — Mira's grandfather, a one-time guardian of the archival stacks, now mysteriously missing.
- The Codex Thief — an elusive antagonist who moves between books stealing and altering rare texts.
- Archivist Lumen — a guardian at the repository of manuscripts who provides research, maps of narrative topology, and occasional moral counsel.

The finished novel must contain all of the following:
- Protagonist is explicitly a third-generation practitioner of a craft that allows travel into novels to enforce rules.
- Multiple distinct novel-worlds/genres must be visited and depicted, with their own internal logic and consequences for the protagonist's methods.
- The protagonist teams up with a beloved fictional detective character (a companion who is itself a character from stories) as a co-investigator.
- The central mystery combines two linked threads: the protagonist's grandfather has vanished and an unusual, powerful book has been stolen from protected archives.
- The narrative explores the tension between legalistic enforcement of story-world order and the protagonist's personal loyalties, including the ethical implications of interfering in fictional lives.

Write the novel itself — no outline, no commentary, no notes to the reader.
