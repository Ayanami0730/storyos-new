- obj-filing-mark: A tiny, distinctive filing nick on a brass plate from the clockwork — a maker's tic that Aldo recognises from Derek's workbench.

### world-rules  <world/rules.yaml>

These govern what is TRUE. They do not say what anyone KNOWS.
A character knows a rule only where canon says so; otherwise they may be ignorant of it, wrong about it, or in the middle of working it out — and if the story is about someone discovering one of these, writing it into their thoughts early is how that story gets destroyed.

- No supernatural solutions or unexplained actions; the locked-room must be resolved by physical mechanism, misdirection, or human action consistent with the environment.
- All clues relied on in the solution must be introduced to the reader (or the narrator) before the reveal scene and have identifiable provenance.
- Times, locations and physical traces must be internally consistent (the watch's stopped time, hatch abrasions, ladder marks, oil stains and filing nick must align with the clockwork reconstruction).
- Forensic detail may be stylised but must not violate basic plausibility (no instantaneous chemical transformations, no impossible mechanical feats).
- The narrator (Aldo) is a competent, essentially reliable detective; he may withhold interpretation but not facts.
- No later scene may introduce new characters, locations, or significant objects not listed above without explicitly adding them to the index with provenance.
